US wins court authority over $1.2 billion in Binance hack crypto

The Justice Department has court authority to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion from Binance’s $6.3 billion hack, while recovery efforts continue.

The Crypto Front Page DeskNov 13, 2026 · 18:30 UTC3 min read

US authorities have court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, after investigators and private firms froze funds and traced their movement. The Justice Department’s announcement on Thursday says the theft happened around Oct. 29 and attributes it to North Korean military hackers the FBI calls TraderTraitor.

How did the recovery reach the courts?

The process has several steps. First, investigators identified cryptocurrency connected to the theft. U.S. agencies then worked with virtual asset service providers, companies that handle crypto transactions, to freeze funds. The Justice Department says those combined government and private-sector efforts have frozen more than $2.7 billion so far.

From that frozen total, the department obtained court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion. That legal step gives authorities power to take control of the assets or prevent their movement while proceedings continue. It does not mean a judge has made a final decision on who should receive them. The Associated Press report says the government typically files a forfeiture claim, after which a judge determines who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds.

Who is still handling the stolen assets?

The FBI, the Justice Department and law enforcement partners are coordinating efforts to recover the crypto. Investigators are also relying on virtual asset service providers to help interdict it. The department says TraderTraitor actors continue to launder assets through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges. These services can move funds between networks or accounts, like parcels passing through a series of transfer hubs, making continued tracing and intervention necessary.

The FBI previously issued an alert attributing the Binance theft and asking virtual asset service providers to block transactions involving addresses used to launder the stolen assets, including transactions derived from those addresses. The Justice Department says work to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the crypto is ongoing.

What does the court authorization change?

The authorization advances the government’s effort to gain legal control of part of the stolen assets; it does not settle the final ownership of that crypto. More than $2.7 billion has been frozen across government and private-sector efforts, while more than $1.2 billion of that amount is covered by the court authority announced Thursday. The original theft was more than $6 billion, according to the Justice Department.

The next steps are further tracing and possible forfeiture proceedings, as authorities and service providers try to stop the funds from moving. The department says those efforts continue; it has not announced a final distribution of recovered assets.
