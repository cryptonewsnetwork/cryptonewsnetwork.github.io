DOJ gets authority over $1.2 billion in Binance theft funds

The Justice Department says it won court authority over more than $1.2 billion of Binance theft funds, advancing recovery while laundering efforts continue.

The Crypto Front Page DeskNov 19, 2026 · 09:53 UTC3 min read

The U.S. Justice Department has court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, while efforts to trace and freeze the remaining funds continue. In its Nov. 12 announcement, the department said the amount is part of more than $2.7 billion frozen through cooperation between the U.S. government and private companies.

How did the stolen crypto reach this stage?

The FBI says members of the North Korean military, whom it calls TraderTraitor, stole more than $6 billion in virtual assets from Binance on or about Oct. 29. After the theft, federal agencies worked with virtual asset service providers to identify and interdict funds. These providers helped freeze assets before they moved beyond law enforcement’s reach, according to the Justice Department.

That process matters because cryptocurrency can move between wallets and services. The FBI’s cyber alert on the Binance attack says investigators observed the hackers converting stolen assets primarily into Ether, with further laundering expected through decentralized finance and instant swap services. The FBI urged service providers to block transactions involving listed addresses or funds derived from them.

Who controls the $1.2 billion?

The Justice Department’s wording is precise: it has court authorization to “seize or restrain” more than $1.2 billion of the frozen funds. That does not establish that the full amount has already been transferred into government-controlled wallets. A restraint can keep assets from moving while legal proceedings continue; a seizure puts property under government control. Like a hold on an account, a restraint limits movement without itself deciding who ultimately owns the money.

The announcement does not specify how much of the $1.2 billion has been seized and how much restrained, or identify the assets covered. The wider $2.7 billion figure is the amount frozen so far by public and private efforts, not the amount the department says it has authority over. Binance is the exchange from which the assets were stolen; the announcement does not say that its customers’ balances were seized.

What changes, and what happens next?

The court authorization gives the government a legal route to pursue part of the stolen assets. It does not conclude recovery or forfeiture. The Justice Department says the work to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the funds is ongoing, and that TraderTraitor actors continue to launder assets through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges.

For crypto services, the immediate operational request is to identify and block transactions linked to the addresses in the FBI alert. The next developments to watch are whether more assets are frozen or seized, and whether forfeiture proceedings establish who can claim them. For now, the changed figure is the portion of the theft covered by court authorization: more than $1.2 billion of more than $2.7 billion frozen.
