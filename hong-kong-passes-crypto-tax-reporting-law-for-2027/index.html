Hong Kong passes crypto tax reporting law for 2027

Hong Kong passed a bill requiring covered crypto providers to collect customer and transaction data from 2027, with tax information exchanges due to start in 2028.

The Crypto Front Page DeskNov 6, 2026 · 09:59 UTC3 min read

Hong Kong’s Legislative Council passed a bill on November 4 requiring covered crypto-asset service providers to collect customer and transaction information from 2027, setting up annual exchanges of tax data with partner jurisdictions from 2028. The Hong Kong government’s announcement says the law implements the OECD’s Crypto-Asset Reporting Framework, or CARF.

The process runs in steps: a covered provider identifies its customers and checks where they are tax resident; it records qualifying crypto activity; then it reports required information to the Inland Revenue Department, which can exchange it with partner tax authorities. The framework is an information channel for tax authorities, not a public record of customer trades.

Which crypto firms and transactions are covered?

The Inland Revenue Department says a provider can be covered if, as a business, it facilitates exchanges between crypto-assets or between crypto-assets and fiat currency for customers, including through a trading platform. Its examples include exchanges, brokers and crypto-asset ATM operators.

CARF covers three broad transaction types: crypto exchanged for fiat currency, one crypto-asset exchanged for another, and transfers of crypto-assets. That last category includes transfers to external wallet addresses. The system works like a reporting chain: a provider collects and submits the information, while the tax department passes relevant data to partner jurisdictions.

What information will providers collect?

Providers must carry out due diligence to identify reportable users and their tax jurisdictions. The Inland Revenue Department’s CARF guidance lists identifying details such as a user’s name, address, tax-residence jurisdiction and taxpayer identification number. For each relevant crypto-asset, providers also report annual totals, including gross amounts paid or received, units and transaction counts.

The reporting applies to users who are tax residents of jurisdictions designated as reportable. For customers opening accounts, providers must obtain a self-certification and check that it appears reasonable. The department says existing users must also provide self-certification within 12 months after the rules begin.

When will reporting and information exchanges begin?

Covered providers will begin collecting information under CARF in 2027. Hong Kong’s Inland Revenue Department is due to start exchanging crypto-transaction information with partner jurisdictions in 2028, according to the government announcement. The exchanges are to be reciprocal and subject to data confidentiality and security.

The department says it will issue guidance and provide technical support to help firms comply. The change is a new reporting path from crypto providers to Hong Kong’s tax authority and, from 2028, to partner tax authorities. The next step to watch is the department’s implementation guidance for providers.
