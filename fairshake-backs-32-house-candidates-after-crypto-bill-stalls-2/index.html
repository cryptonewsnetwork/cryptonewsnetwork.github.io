Fairshake backs 32 House candidates after crypto bill stalls

Fairshake will back 32 House incumbents, including six with $1 million in outside spending, as stalled Senate crypto rules push the industry toward November races.

The Crypto Front Page DeskOct 13, 2026 · 20:52 UTC3 min read

Fairshake plans to support 32 House incumbents in November’s midterm elections, including six who will each receive $1 million in campaign spending, as the crypto industry looks to the next Congress after market-structure legislation stalled in the Senate. The bipartisan slate includes 19 Republicans and 13 Democrats, according to The Associated Press’s report on Fairshake’s announcement. All 32 supported the legislation, which aimed to establish a federal framework for digital-asset markets.

How does Fairshake’s $1 million support work?

Fairshake’s announcement describes campaign spending, rather than money deposited into the candidates’ campaign accounts. The group says it is a federal independent expenditure-only committee and supports candidates through independent activities, as Fairshake’s own description of its committee explains. In practice, that means the committee can spend on its own election activity to support a candidate. The six disclosed allocations are for that outside spending; the group did not disclose how much it plans to spend on the other 26 races.

The six candidates named for $1 million each are Democrats Janelle Bynum of Oregon, Steven Horsford of Nevada and Derek Tran of California, and Republicans French Hill of Arkansas, Bryan Steil of Wisconsin and Bill Huizenga of Michigan. The remaining slate includes senior House lawmakers from both parties. Fairshake spokesperson Geoff Vetter said the group backs candidates who support crypto in both parties.

What happened to the legislation the candidates supported?

The House passed the market-structure bill in July 2025. It would set rules for how digital-asset markets operate and how they are overseen. The bill then remained in the Senate for more than a year. Negotiators did not reach agreement on ethics safeguards and other provisions, and the Senate blocked it in September on a bipartisan procedural vote, Roll Call reported.

The stall leaves the industry’s push for federal crypto rules unfinished. Fairshake is now backing lawmakers who supported the bill, while seeking to shape the makeup of the next Congress that could revisit it. That is the link between the legislative fight and the spending slate: support for candidates is routed through a political committee’s independent activity, while the bill itself must still clear Congress.

What is known about Fairshake’s next spending?

The group has not said how much it will spend across all 32 House races or whether it will add more House or Senate spending before November. AP reported that Fairshake and its affiliated PACs had about $120 million in cash on hand at the end of August. The group has also announced a separate plan to spend nearly $30 million opposing Democratic Senate candidate Sherrod Brown in Ohio.

What changed is that Fairshake has named a bipartisan slate and disclosed six initial $1 million commitments after the Senate bill stalled. The next details to watch are its reported spending in the other races and whether Congress takes up market-structure legislation again.
