How to Restake stETH With a Hardware Wallet

To set up a hardware wallet to restake ETH, connect Ledger or Trezor through MetaMask or Rabby, then approve, deposit and delegate an LST on-device.

The Crypto Front Page DeskNov 19, 2026 · 18:50 UTC4 min read

An LST restake uses three separate signed actions: token approval, deposit, then delegation. To set up a hardware wallet to restake ETH, you connect Ledger or Trezor through a browser wallet and confirm each action on the device.

Have the Ethereum app installed, ETH for gas, and an LST such as stETH. The app may show no restaked balance until the deposit is delegated. The eigen layer zero-balance view article explains how to trace that state in the eigen layer app.

Restake an LST with your hardware wallet

To restake an LST, prepare the device, connect it to the app, then approve, deposit and delegate in order. Native restaking needs an Ethereum validator; this walkthrough covers the LST route.

  1. Update the device and open Ethereum. Start with your initialized Ledger or Trezor holding ETH. Update its firmware, install the Ethereum app and open it. Keep ETH available in the account for transaction fees. On Ledger, the Ethereum app may ask you to allow blind signing for contract data it cannot decode. Enable it only for this session. It allows the device to sign a contract call, so it makes checking the destination address on the device screen especially important.
  2. Connect through MetaMask or Rabby. Pair the hardware wallet with MetaMask or Rabby in your browser. Confirm that the account address shown in the wallet matches the address displayed on the device. This is the address that will send the transactions and hold the resulting position.
  3. Connect to the app. Open the eigen layer app and connect the same hardware-wallet account through your browser wallet. Review the connected address before starting. A browser connection lets the app prepare transactions; the private keys stay on the hardware wallet.
  4. Approve the LST. Choose stETH or another supported LST and start the deposit flow. The first transaction gives the contract permission to use the token. Read the transaction details and destination contract address on the device screen before approving. Treat that address like the recipient on an envelope: the browser’s display alone is not the device’s confirmation.
  5. Sign the deposit. After the approval confirms, submit the deposit as a separate transaction. Confirm it on the hardware wallet too. Say you are restaking stETH: the token approval permits the app’s contract to use it, and the deposit moves it into the restaking position. Each contract call needs its own physical confirmation.
  6. Choose an operator and delegate. Select an operator in the app and submit the delegation transaction. Confirm that transaction on the device. The deposit and delegation are distinct steps; the deposited LST is not delegated until this final transaction confirms.
  7. Turn off blind signing. When the transactions are complete, disable blind signing in the Ledger Ethereum app. Leaving it on beyond the session keeps contract calls signable without the device decoding their data. Before every approval, deposit or delegation, verify the destination address shown on the hardware wallet screen.
  8. Confirm the result. Find the deposit and delegation transactions on Etherscan using the device address. Wait for both to show as confirmed. The restaked and delegated position should then appear in the app; the Etherscan records tie those actions to the address you checked on the device.

Related

Can I restake from a Safe multisig?

A Safe can be used only when the app and the Safe’s transaction flow support the relevant deposit and delegation calls. In that setup, the hardware wallet signs its owner’s approval through the Safe flow; confirm the transaction details and destination on the device before signing.

Does a hardware wallet protect me from slashing?

No. A hardware wallet keeps signing keys on the device and requires physical confirmation, but it does not remove slashing risk from restaking. Your operator choice and the services secured by the protocol still matter. After delegation, keep track of the position and the operator you selected.
