EU plan would widen Russia sanctions to crypto networks

The Commission proposed a 22nd Russia sanctions package targeting crypto and payment networks; all 27 EU members must approve it before the measures can take effect.

The Crypto Front Page DeskNov 24, 2026 · 00:59 UTC3 min read

The European Commission’s proposed 22nd Russia sanctions package would target crypto and payment networks, alongside banks and other sectors. In its statement on 23 November, Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said the measures would focus on Russia and on third countries helping it evade existing sanctions.

How would the proposal reach crypto networks?

The Commission has not named specific crypto platforms or set out a detailed rule for transactions in its public statement. It says the package would crack down on crypto and payment networks, and that third countries helping Russia evade sanctions would be included. The proposed focus is on the routes and services that could help bypass restrictions, rather than a stated blanket ban on crypto use.

To support that work, the Commission says it has launched a Crypto Sanctions Task Force. The statement does not specify the task force’s membership, powers or timetable. Its role, as described, is to support efforts against the targeted networks and coordinate attention to circumvention with international partners.

What changes for Russian banks?

The proposal would extend transaction bans to about 60 Russian banks and remove all 60 from SWIFT, the messaging system used for international payments. SWIFT carries payment instructions between financial institutions; removing a bank from the network restricts that channel, while the proposed transaction ban would separately bar covered dealings with the listed banks. The Commission has not named the banks in the statement.

These measures sit alongside the crypto provisions because both address financial routes the EU says can be used to fund Russia’s war or evade sanctions. The package also proposes energy measures, including a wider ban on services for Russian energy infrastructure, as well as restrictions on Russian participation in EU sports and cultural events.

When could the measures take effect?

They are proposals, not rules already in force. The Associated Press report on the package says all 27 EU member countries must endorse it before it can take effect; the process can take weeks, and measures may change. The public statement does not give a date for a vote or say whether members have agreed to the package.

For crypto businesses and users, the practical consequence will depend on the final text: which services and transactions are covered, how third-country links are handled, and when any restrictions begin. The change so far is a Commission proposal to widen sanctions enforcement into crypto and payment networks. The next development to watch is whether member states approve it and what specific rules the final package contains.
