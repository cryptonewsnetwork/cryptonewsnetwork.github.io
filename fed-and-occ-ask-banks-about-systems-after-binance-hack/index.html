Fed and OCC ask banks about systems after Binance hack

The Fed and OCC asked banks about unusual activity after Binance’s $6.3 billion hack, turning a crypto breach into a check on possible links to banking.

The Crypto Front Page DeskNov 9, 2026 · 03:38 UTC3 min read

The Federal Reserve and Office of the Comptroller of the Currency contacted banks after Binance’s $6.3 billion hack, asking whether systems showed unusual activity or service disruption as regulators checked for possible spillover into traditional finance, according to Reuters’ report published Nov. 5. The outreach began Monday morning, several days after the breach. It put bank supervisors, individual lenders and Binance into the same sequence of checks: ask about disruptions, inspect systems for similar exposure, then decide whether any fixes are needed.

What did the Fed and OCC ask banks to check?

Supervisors asked banks about unusual activity and disruptions to their services, Reuters reported, citing people familiar with the calls. One contacted bank reported no service disruption. Its employees prepared talking points in case customers asked about the hack, but the bank had received few inquiries by the time Reuters published its report.

That account describes a check for signs of impact, not a finding that the breach crossed into bank systems. The Federal Reserve and OCC declined to comment to Reuters. Binance said it was unaware of the conversations between bankers and regulators, or whether the hack had affected US banks. A company spokesperson said Binance was in contact with regulators around the world to keep them informed about the security breach.

How are banks examining the breach?

Some banks are running their own diagnostics to test whether their systems could withstand a similar attack, Reuters reported. Within hours of the incident, a cybersecurity team at one of the largest US banks contacted counterparts at Binance. The bank was preparing an internal report that would lead to a review of its systems and could lead to cybersecurity updates. Reuters said it was not clear whether the bank was vulnerable to the tactics used against Binance.

The steps resemble a building manager checking neighboring properties after an alarm: first look for signs of trouble, then inspect for the same weak points. Here, regulators’ questions and banks’ technical reviews are separate parts of the response. The report did not identify the banks contacted or describe the attackers’ method, so it does not establish that the same weakness exists in crypto and bank infrastructure.

What is known about the Binance attack?

Binance said hackers stole more than $6 billion. The FBI attributed the theft to North Korean hackers from a group known as TraderTraitor, while North Korea denied responsibility. The Associated Press reported the FBI attribution and North Korea’s denial in its account of the dispute. Reuters described the hack as $6.3 billion; those figures refer to the reported scale of the breach, not losses at banks.

The immediate change is that US bank supervisors have asked lenders to check for suspicious activity after a major crypto theft, while banks conduct their own reviews. The next facts to watch are whether any bank reports disruption, whether reviews identify shared vulnerabilities, and whether Binance or regulators publish further details about how the breach occurred.
