Deutsche Bank custody service goes live for paying clients

Deutsche Bank's custody service is live for paying European clients, holding keys for selected crypto assets as it explores tokenized products.

The Crypto Front Page DeskNov 12, 2026 · 05:53 UTC3 min read

Deutsche Bank has put its digital asset custody service live for paying clients, giving institutions in Europe a bank-run way to hold and transfer selected crypto assets while the bank explores tokenized products with its asset manager. The service manages clients’ wallets and private keys: those keys authorize control of assets on a blockchain, so custody means securing the keys as well as arranging transfers. Reuters reported the launch on Nov. 11, following the bank’s September plan to offer custody in Europe.

What does the custody service do with a client’s assets?

It manages the wallets and private keys that clients use to access and transfer their assets. Deutsche Bank’s service initially supports Bitcoin and Ether, plus selected stablecoins or e-money tokens including USDC, EURC and EURAU, according to Deutsche Bank’s custody service announcement. Clients can hold those assets with the bank and transfer them to other parties, The Block reported.

The process starts when keys are created and protected with dedicated hardware. The bank separates responsibilities among staff, so more than one person must approve important actions. It also keeps cryptographic key material in separate storage environments, including offline cold storage, and maintains backup and recovery arrangements. Think of it as a safe that requires multiple authorized people to open, with spare access arrangements if the main route fails. The bank’s custody setup includes warm and cold storage, according to The Block.

What does Deutsche Asset Management’s agreement cover?

The two firms signed a memorandum of understanding to explore collaboration on tokenized money market funds, crypto-asset exchange-traded commodities and stablecoins. The Block reported that the potential work could include custody and settlement, liquidity management, treasury and distribution using Deutsche Bank’s custody platform. The agreement is exploratory: it sets areas for the firms to examine, rather than announcing that these products are already available.

That link between custody and asset management matters because issuing a tokenized fund involves more than representing fund interests digitally. The firms would also need arrangements for holding the underlying digital assets or tokens, processing transfers and managing liquidity. The agreement identifies these work areas but does not specify a launch date or confirm that a tokenized fund has been issued.

What changes for clients now, and what comes next?

Paying clients can now use Deutsche Bank’s service to hold and transfer the supported assets, subject to the bank’s client and product processes. The bank has said tokenized financial instruments may be added later, subject to client demand, product approval, risk management and regulatory processes. Its stated medium-term aim is a regulated system spanning custody, tokenization and settlement.

The change is that custody has moved from a planned offering to a live service, alongside an exploratory agreement with the asset manager. The next markers are whether the firms turn that agreement into specific products and whether Deutsche Bank expands the assets its service supports.
