Fairshake backs 32 House incumbents after crypto bill stalls

Fairshake will back 32 House incumbents, including six with $1 million each, as the crypto industry seeks lawmakers to revive stalled market-structure legislation.

The Crypto Front Page DeskOct 8, 2026 · 11:51 UTC3 min read

Fairshake, a major crypto campaign group, said on Oct. 5 it will support 32 House incumbents in the November midterms: 19 Republicans and 13 Democrats, according to the Associated Press report. All 32 backed the industry’s priority market-structure bill, which passed the House but stalled in the Senate.

The spending plan has two disclosed parts. Fairshake named a slate of 32 candidates, then identified six who will each receive $1 million in support: Democrats Janelle Bynum of Oregon, Steven Horsford of Nevada and Derek Tran of California; and Republicans French Hill of Arkansas, Bryan Steil of Wisconsin and Bill Huizenga of Michigan. The group did not disclose how much it plans to spend on the other 26.

Why is Fairshake targeting these House members?

The candidates share a vote for the Digital Asset Market Clarity Act, which would set federal rules for digital-asset markets and divide oversight between regulators. The House passed the bill in July 2025. It then spent more than a year in the Senate, where lawmakers did not reach agreement on ethics safeguards, among other issues. The Senate blocked it in September on a procedural vote.

That sequence links the legislative fight to the campaign plan: the bill stalled, and Fairshake is directing election support to lawmakers who backed it. Roll Call reported that all six of the named recipients sit on the House Financial Services Committee, which handles financial regulation. Its account also says no House Republican voted against the Clarity Act, while the supported Democrats were among the 78 in their caucus who voted for it.

How much will the group spend?

The six disclosed commitments total $6 million. That is a floor for the House effort, not its full price: Fairshake has not specified spending for the other 26 candidates. It reported about $120 million in cash on hand with affiliated PACs at the end of August, according to AP.

Fairshake’s support is part of a wider political operation. The group and its affiliates spent $40 million backing 29 Democrats and $31 million backing 28 Republicans in primaries and special elections this year, its spokesperson told AP. Separately, Fairshake plans to spend nearly $30 million opposing Democratic Senate candidate Sherrod Brown in Ohio.

What happens to the bill after the midterms?

Fairshake says it wants to build support for crypto legislation in the next Congress. The election slate does not determine whether a bill advances: lawmakers would still need to negotiate its terms and secure the votes required in each chamber. The immediate change is a disclosed $6 million in planned support for six House incumbents, within a 32-candidate slate. Watch for Fairshake to disclose additional spending and for the next Congress to take up market-structure legislation.
