US gets court authority over $1.2B in stolen Binance crypto

US authorities say providers have frozen $2.7 billion from Binance’s $6.3 billion theft, with court approval to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion.

The Crypto Front Page DeskNov 16, 2026 · 12:48 UTC3 min read

U.S. authorities are working with virtual asset service providers to freeze and seize cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, the Justice Department said Nov. 12, reporting court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion. The Justice Department announcement describes the sequence: trace the assets, block their movement through service providers, then seek legal control of funds. The government and private sector have frozen more than $2.7 billion in total from the theft.

How did authorities move against the stolen assets?

The FBI attributes the theft to North Korean military members it calls TraderTraitor. The Justice Department says the theft occurred on or about Oct. 29 and involved more than $6 billion in virtual assets; the Associated Press reported the amount as about $6.3 billion.

After the theft, U.S. agencies and law enforcement partners worked with virtual asset service providers, or VASPs, to interdict the assets. These companies can help stop transfers involving assets they handle. In practical terms, the process resembles asking intermediaries to hold a parcel while authorities establish the legal basis to take control of it.

The government says TraderTraitor actors have continued laundering funds through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges. Bridges move assets between blockchain networks; mixers can obscure transaction trails; exchanges provide another point through which assets may pass. Investigators are tracing those movements while providers block transactions linked to the addresses used in the laundering, as the FBI urged in a separate cyber alert.

Who does the action affect?

The theft directly affected Binance, whose exchange was the source of the stolen assets. The enforcement effort also involves service providers that may encounter transfers connected to the stolen cryptocurrency. The FBI has asked those providers to block transactions with, or derived from, addresses TraderTraitor actors are using to launder the funds.

The Justice Department describes more than $2.7 billion as frozen through combined government and private-sector efforts so far. More than $1.2 billion of that amount has court authorization for seizure or restraint. Those figures describe different stages: a freeze limits movement, while court authorization gives the government legal authority to pursue control of specified assets.

What changes with court authorization?

The authorization allows the government to pursue seizure or restraint of the identified funds. It does not, by itself, establish that the assets have been returned to Binance or to other claimants. As the Associated Press reported, the government typically files a forfeiture claim with a judge, who then determines who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds.

The amount under court authority is therefore only part of the recovery process. The Justice Department says tracing, freezing, seizure and forfeiture efforts remain underway as the funds move through crypto services. The next developments to watch are whether further assets are frozen and how courts handle claims to funds the government recovers.
