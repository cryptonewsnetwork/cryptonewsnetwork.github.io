OCC rule leaves stablecoin rewards open for crypto platforms

The OCC’s GENIUS Act rule bars stablecoin issuers from paying interest but leaves exchange rewards to case-by-case review, with the rule taking effect in January.

The Crypto Front Page DeskOct 26, 2026 · 07:23 UTC3 min read

The Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC) issued a final rule on Oct. 21 to implement the GENIUS Act, leaving crypto platforms able to offer interest-like rewards on stablecoin balances while barring issuers from paying holders interest directly. The Associated Press’s report says exchanges can pass along earnings from stablecoins to customers on a case-by-case basis.

The rule governs payment stablecoin issuers under OCC jurisdiction and some related custody activities. The agency says it takes effect Jan. 18, 2027. That leaves a distinction between the company that issues a token and the platform where customers hold or trade it.

How can an exchange pay rewards if issuers cannot pay interest?

A stablecoin is designed to hold a steady value, often around $1. Its issuer backs the tokens with reserve assets such as bank deposits or U.S. Treasury securities, which can earn interest. The issuer cannot pay that interest to people simply for holding its stablecoin, under the GENIUS Act framework described by the AP.

An exchange or other platform can sit between the issuer and the customer. It may share earnings tied to stablecoin balances as a reward. In plain terms, the issuer holds the reservoir of assets; a separate platform decides whether to pass some of the return to its users. The OCC’s approach leaves those arrangements for case-by-case consideration rather than prohibiting them outright.

What does the OCC rule cover?

The OCC’s final rule bulletin sets requirements for payment stablecoin issuance and related activities, including reserve assets, redemption, risk management, reporting and supervision. The agency says the rule applies to national banks and subsidiaries, federal savings associations, certain foreign issuers, and other issuers subject to its authority under the GENIUS Act.

The rule does not establish that every rewards program is permitted. The AP reported that platforms may offer rewards on a case-by-case basis; the OCC bulletin describes the broad framework and its effective date, but does not spell out a blanket approval for exchange programs. The OCC says separate rulemaking will address Bank Secrecy Act, anti-money-laundering and sanctions requirements.

What happens before the rule takes effect?

The OCC’s rule takes effect Jan. 18, 2027. Until then, the practical question is how the agency will assess arrangements between stablecoin issuers and platforms, and what conditions it will apply to rewards programs. The rule leaves the issuer’s direct interest payments barred while allowing platform rewards to remain under case-by-case review.
