Binance says hot-wallet breach moved $6.34bn in assets

Binance says an attacker moved $6.34bn through its hot-wallet signing system; the exchange has replenished 98% and is reopening withdrawals in phases.

The Crypto Front Page DeskNov 5, 2026 · 11:14 UTC3 min read

Binance says an attacker used its hot-wallet signing system to move about $6.34 billion in digital assets on October 29, a breach that led the exchange to suspend withdrawals while it contained the transfers. In its initial findings and response update, published November 3, Binance described how the attacker gained access to a wallet service and authorized transactions outside the normal withdrawal process.

How did the attacker authorize the transfers?

Binance’s account starts with unauthorized access to an internal wallet-signing service. That service authorizes transactions from hot wallets, which hold crypto for operational use and withdrawals. The attacker used the service to initiate transfers across several networks, including XRP, Solana, Optimism, Bitcoin and Ethereum.

Binance says the attacker then exploited Ethereum’s EIP-7702 authorization mechanism. It allows an account to delegate certain execution rights to a smart contract without revealing its private key. In this case, the attacker used it to give a malicious contract partial execution rights over a Binance hot wallet. That meant Binance’s regular withdrawal controls could not, by themselves, stop further on-chain transfers once the authorization was in place.

The exchange says its hot wallets use multi-party computation, or MPC, so no single private key could simply cancel the authorization. Binance had to send further transactions to revoke it. The final unauthorized transfer completed at 23:56 UTC on October 29; Binance says it cleared the malicious authorization at 00:10 UTC on October 30. It has reported no evidence that wallet private keys were leaked.

What is Binance doing about the lost assets?

Binance says it will cover the impact from its Secure Asset Fund for Users and corporate treasury. By its November 3 update, it said approximately $2.85 billion had been frozen or intercepted. In a November 4 recovery update, the exchange said it had replenished about 98% of the impacted amount and had set aside more than the funds needed to cover the losses. It said the remaining 2% would take longer to buy because those tokens are less liquid.

Which services are returning?

Binance’s November 5 update says withdrawals for more assets are reopening in phases after security and readiness checks; deposits remain open. Not every listed asset becomes available at once, and Binance says it will add more as checks are completed.

The change is from emergency containment to a staged restoration of withdrawals, while asset replacement and the investigation continue. Binance says it is preparing further technical findings and a detailed post-incident report; the remaining questions are how the access was obtained and what safeguards will prevent a repeat.
