US secures court authority over $1.2bn in Binance theft

The Justice Department has court authority to seize or restrain over $1.2 billion of crypto stolen from Binance, while tracing and recovery efforts continue.

The Crypto Front Page DeskNov 15, 2026 · 01:41 UTC3 min read

The US Justice Department has obtained court authority to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, as investigators and private companies continue tracing the funds. The department said the theft took place on or about October 29 and involved more than $6 billion in virtual assets. In its announcement on the Binance theft, the Justice Department said government and private-sector efforts had frozen more than $2.7 billion so far.

How did authorities get control of the funds?

The FBI attributes the theft to North Korean military hackers it calls TraderTraitor. After the funds left Binance, US authorities worked with virtual asset service providers, or VASPs, to trace and block their movement. VASPs include businesses that handle cryptocurrency transactions. The FBI’s earlier alert identified addresses linked to the activity and urged service providers to block transactions involving those addresses or funds derived from them.

A freeze restricts movement of assets; it does not by itself transfer ownership to the government. The Justice Department says it has court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion of the frozen total. Those are legal steps toward taking control of property, while the wider tracing and freezing effort continues.

Who is affected by the seizure order?

The assets were reported stolen from Binance, the exchange targeted in the attack. The FBI said the hackers had converted stolen assets primarily into Ether and warned that they could move them through decentralized finance services and instant swap services as part of further laundering. The Justice Department says TraderTraitor actors continue to launder assets through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges.

Those services can move tokens between blockchains, exchange one asset for another, or obscure the path between transactions. That creates multiple points where investigators and service providers may try to identify or stop funds. The FBI’s request to block transactions with listed addresses extends the response beyond Binance to companies that process, route or analyze crypto transactions.

What does court authorization change?

It gives the government legal authority to pursue the specified assets through seizure or restraint, but the Justice Department’s announcement does not say that all of the more than $1.2 billion has been transferred to government control or returned to Binance. The Associated Press report on the recovery process says the government typically files a forfeiture claim with a judge, who then determines who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds.

The immediate change is that a court-authorized process now covers more than $1.2 billion of the funds identified in the broader freeze. The next steps are continued tracing, efforts to prevent further transfers, and legal proceedings over forfeiture and any claims to the assets.
