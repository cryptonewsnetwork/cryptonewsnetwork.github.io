US Gets Court Authority to Seize $1.2B in Binance Crypto

The Justice Department has court authority to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in crypto stolen from Binance, while tracing of the wider theft continues.

The Crypto Front Page DeskNov 22, 2026 · 00:31 UTC3 min read

The U.S. Justice Department says it has court authority to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, a step in a wider effort to freeze and recover the funds. In its November 12 announcement, the department said government and private-sector work had frozen more than $2.7 billion of the stolen assets so far.

How did authorities move from the theft to a court order?

The sequence began with a theft from Binance on or around October 29. The Justice Department said North Korean military members, whom the FBI calls TraderTraitor, took more than $6 billion in virtual assets. The FBI had attributed the theft to the group before the seizure announcement.

After the theft, U.S. agencies worked with virtual asset service providers, or VASPs, to identify and freeze assets. The DOJ says the stolen funds continued to move through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges. Those services can move assets between networks or wallets, so investigators and providers must follow the trail as it shifts.

A freeze restricts movement of assets while investigators pursue the next steps. The court authorization covers more than $1.2 billion of the funds already frozen; it does not mean that amount has been returned to Binance or to customers. The Associated Press report notes that the government typically files a forfeiture claim with a judge, who then decides who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds. That process is like placing a hold on property while a court determines who is entitled to it.

Who is affected by the asset freeze?

The direct target is the cryptocurrency attributed to the Binance theft. The DOJ did not identify the assets by token, blockchain or wallet in its announcement. It said U.S. agencies and private-sector partners had frozen more than $2.7 billion collectively, with more than $1.2 billion of that total covered by court authority to seize or restrain.

The FBI has also asked VASPs to block transactions with, or derived from, cryptocurrency addresses TraderTraitor actors are using to launder the stolen Binance assets. That request puts exchanges and other providers in the path between a traceable address and a transaction that could move funds further.

What happens next in the recovery effort?

The Justice Department says tracing, freezing, seizing and forfeiting the assets is ongoing. Those steps have different consequences: freezing limits movement, seizure brings assets under government control, and forfeiture resolves legal ownership through court proceedings.

The announcement changes the legal status of more than $1.2 billion within the larger frozen pool. The next developments to watch are whether authorities obtain forfeiture and whether the funds can ultimately be returned to rightful claimants. The DOJ has not said when that process will conclude.
