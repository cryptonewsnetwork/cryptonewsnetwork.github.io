Three Bridge Cost Checks Before You Move Crypto

Before moving crypto, compare the source-chain transaction, the bridge route’s fee and exchange rate, and the gas or liquidity needed after arrival.

The Crypto Front Page DeskOct 22, 2026 · 14:24 UTC4 min read

Before moving crypto across chains, check the cost to send it, the cost of the bridge route, and what you will need to use it after it arrives. A bridge coordinates activity on two networks: one transaction begins on the source chain, a bridge mechanism processes the transfer, and a destination-side transaction or release makes funds available on the other chain. The steps and fees vary by route, so a single headline fee rarely tells the whole story.

Start with the asset and networks you intend to use, then compare the amount you send with the amount the route says you will receive. If you want to understand the transfer mechanics for one example, read how the Manta bridge moves assets. The same cost checks apply across bridges, though the steps behind them can differ.

What does the source-chain transaction cost?

The source-chain cost is the network fee for starting the transfer, plus any separate transaction needed to authorize the bridge to use your token. Wallets often call this fee gas. It is paid to the source network, usually in that network’s native token, rather than being part of the amount sent through the bridge.

Some routes require an approval transaction before the transfer. If you have not approved that token for the bridge contract, the wallet may ask you to sign one transaction for approval and another for the transfer. Each can carry its own network fee. Other routes or assets may not require a separate approval, so inspect the wallet prompts instead of assuming there will be one or two.

Check the fee estimate for each requested transaction and make sure your wallet holds enough of the source network’s gas token. A transfer can fail to start if you hold the asset you want to bridge but lack the token needed to pay the network fee. Network fees can also change between quote and confirmation, so review the final wallet screen before signing.

How much will the bridge route take from the transfer?

The bridge route can reduce the amount received through an explicit fee, a token conversion, or a less favorable exchange rate. Some bridges lock an asset on one chain and release or create a corresponding asset on another. Others use available liquidity to deliver funds on the destination chain. The route determines which costs and timing apply.

Compare the route’s quoted receive amount with the amount you are sending, and check whether the quote identifies a bridge fee or exchange rate. If the route swaps tokens, the quoted amount may also reflect slippage: the difference between the expected exchange and the exchange that executes. A route with a low visible fee can still return less if its exchange rate is worse.

  • Record the amount sent and the estimated amount received.
  • Check whether a conversion happens along the route and which token you will receive.
  • Look for a minimum receive amount and the conditions that could prevent execution.
  • Compare routes using the same asset, networks, and transfer amount.

These figures are easiest to compare in the same unit, such as the destination token you expect to receive. If one route quotes a fee in a different token, account for that cost before comparing. A quote is an estimate of a particular route at that moment; it is not a promise that every step will complete at the same cost.

What will it cost to use the funds after arrival?

After the transfer, you may need destination-chain gas to move, swap, or withdraw the received asset. That fee is separate from the source transaction and the bridge route. Check which token arrives, whether it is the version accepted by your intended app, and whether you already have enough of the destination network’s gas token for the next action.

For example, receiving a bridged token does not automatically provide the gas token needed to trade it. You may need to fund the destination wallet first, which can add another transfer and another fee. A bridge may also offer a route that delivers a different asset than the one you planned to use; check the displayed token and network before confirming.

Before sending, review the full sequence: source fee, any approval, bridge quote and expected receive amount, then destination gas and asset usability. The cost that matters is what it takes to get usable funds where you need them. Once you have checked those steps, the remaining things to watch are changes in the route quote and the token and network shown in the final wallet confirmation.
