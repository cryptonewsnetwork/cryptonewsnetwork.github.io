US wins court authority over $1.2bn in Binance theft

The Justice Department says courts authorised seizure or restraint of $1.2 billion from the $2.7 billion frozen after Binance’s $6.3 billion theft, as tracing continues.

The Crypto Front Page DeskNov 15, 2026 · 07:10 UTC3 min read

US authorities have court authorisation to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion of cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, after government agencies and private firms froze more than $2.7 billion in total. The Associated Press reported the FBI’s announcement on Thursday, November 12; the Justice Department’s account makes clear that the $1.2 billion is part of the larger frozen amount.

How did the stolen Binance crypto come under court control?

The Justice Department says the theft happened on or about October 29, when North Korean military members identified by the FBI as TraderTraitor stole more than $6 billion in virtual assets from Binance. The AP reported the theft at about $6.3 billion.

After the theft, federal investigators worked with virtual asset service providers, or VASPs, to intercept the funds. These providers include businesses that handle crypto transfers. The department says the joint effort had frozen more than $2.7 billion by November 12. A freeze holds assets in place; like a hold on an account, it can stop movement without deciding who ultimately owns the funds.

The next legal step is seizure or restraint under court authority. Those terms do not mean a final decision has been made to return the assets to a particular claimant. The AP reports that the government typically files a forfeiture claim with a judge, who then considers who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds.

Who is involved, and where are the funds moving?

The FBI attributes the Binance theft to TraderTraitor, which the Justice Department describes as members of North Korea’s military. Investigators are working with VASPs to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the assets. According to the department’s November 12 announcement, TraderTraitor actors continue to launder funds through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges.

Those services can move crypto between networks, combine funds or enable trades. The FBI has urged VASPs to block transactions involving addresses linked to the stolen assets, including transactions derived from those addresses. That makes the providers part of the response: investigators identify addresses, then ask firms that can control or process transactions to stop related funds from moving.

What changes now, and what remains to watch?

The court authorisation gives the government legal authority over more than $1.2 billion within the amount already frozen. It does not establish that all $2.7 billion has been recovered or that the assets have been returned. The Justice Department says efforts to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the stolen crypto are ongoing.

The next steps are whether investigators can restrain more of the funds as they move through services, and how forfeiture proceedings determine claims to assets under court control. The confirmed change is legal authority over a large portion of the frozen funds; the pursuit of the remaining stolen assets continues.
