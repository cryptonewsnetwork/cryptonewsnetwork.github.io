US authorizes seizure of $1.2 billion in Binance hack funds

The Justice Department has court authority to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion of Binance hack funds, part of a wider freeze still being traced.

The Crypto Front Page DeskNov 17, 2026 · 06:31 UTC3 min read

The US Justice Department has court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, as investigators and private-sector partners continue tracing funds from the exchange hack. The department said the assets are part of more than $2.7 billion frozen so far; that figure describes funds held in place, not money already returned to victims. In its November 12 announcement, the department said the theft took place on or about October 29 and involved more than $6 billion in virtual assets.

How did investigators move from a freeze to court authority?

The process has several steps. First, law enforcement traced assets linked to the theft and worked with virtual asset service providers to freeze more than $2.7 billion. A freeze restricts movement, much like a hold placed on an account, but does not by itself settle who owns the funds. The Justice Department then obtained court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion of that frozen total.

The department says the stolen assets are still being laundered through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges. Those services can move assets between networks or wallets, making coordination with providers important: the FBI has urged them to block transactions involving addresses used by the alleged hackers, or funds derived from those addresses. The announcement does not detail which tokens or individual wallets make up the $1.2 billion.

Who does the FBI say was behind the Binance theft?

The Justice Department says members of the North Korean military, whom the FBI calls TraderTraitor, stole the assets. The FBI had previously attributed the attack to that group. The department says its National Security Division, Criminal Division and the US Attorney’s Office for the District of Columbia worked with law enforcement partners and virtual asset service providers to interdict the funds.

The $1.2 billion action is separate from the same-day disruption of cloud infrastructure linked to FaceStealer, another North Korea-linked campaign. The Justice Department says FaceStealer involved social engineering and was associated with more than $400 million in cryptocurrency theft over the previous 18 months. It did not identify that campaign as the method used in the Binance hack.

Does the authorization mean the money has been returned?

No. The authorization allows the government to pursue seizure or restraint; it does not establish that the full amount has been recovered or returned. The Associated Press reported that the government typically files a forfeiture claim with a judge, who determines whether anyone has a legitimate claim to the assets.

The department says tracing, freezing, seizure and forfeiture efforts remain ongoing. The next concrete steps are whether additional funds can be held, what assets are included in forfeiture proceedings, and whether a court determines who can claim them.
