Zcash activates NU7, cutting target block time to 25 seconds

Zcash activated NU7 at block 3,543,405, targeting 25-second blocks and capping shielded activity to speed payments while reducing wallet scan work.

The Crypto Front Page DeskNov 7, 2026 · 09:01 UTC3 min read

Zcash activated its NU7 network upgrade at block 3,543,405, reducing the target time between blocks from 75 seconds to 25 and changing how private transactions and fees are handled. The Block reported the activation on Nov. 5; CoinDesk’s report on NU7 going live records the block at 02:34 UTC on Nov. 6.

Zcash’s shielded payments hide the sender, recipient and amount. With blocks targeted three times as often, a payment could receive its first confirmation sooner. CoinDesk estimates an exchange that waits for three confirmations could see the expected wait fall from nearly four minutes to about 75 seconds. Each exchange or merchant sets its own confirmation rules, so actual availability depends on the service.

How does NU7 make blocks arrive faster?

Miners gather transactions into blocks, and other miners must receive and check each block before building on it. Shorter intervals leave less time for that exchange; competing blocks can be discarded if miners do not stay in sync. To address this, Zcash node software Zakura reuses parts of a transaction check when the same transaction later appears in a block. It still repeats checks that rely on current balances and chain history.

In a stress test across 80 computers in 11 regions, with blocks full of transactions, 3.43% of blocks were discarded at the 25-second pace, compared with 1.57% at 75 seconds, CoinDesk reported. After activation, the first 225 blocks averaged about 39 seconds apart as mining difficulty adjusted, so the target interval did not mean every block arrived exactly 25 seconds later.

What does NU7 change for shielded payments?

The upgrade limits how much shielded transaction activity can fit in a block. Wallets scan the chain to find payments addressed to them; limiting activity reduces the amount of data they may need to download and check. The NU7 specification estimates the worst-case daily download burden falls from 271 megabytes to 169. With more frequent blocks as well, it estimates capacity for simple shielded payments could rise from about three per second to more than six.

There is a trade-off in Zcash’s oldest pool: NU7 disables version 4 transactions, which Sprout relies on. About 12,224 ZEC remained there at activation and can no longer be spent normally, according to CoinDesk. The upgrade also directs 60% of transaction fees to a reserve and gives miners the remaining 40%; the reserve is scheduled to begin returning through block rewards in 2031. The target block interval and confirmation pace are now changing, while services determine when they credit payments. The next checks are how block timing settles and how wallets and exchanges handle the new rules.
