US authorizes restraint of $1.2 billion in stolen Binance crypto

The Justice Department has court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in stolen Binance crypto, part of a wider effort to freeze the funds.

The Crypto Front Page DeskNov 21, 2026 · 14:37 UTC3 min read

US authorities have court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, the Justice Department said on Nov. 12. The amount is part of more than $2.7 billion in stolen assets that government agencies and private-sector partners have frozen since the theft, according to the Justice Department announcement.

How did authorities stop some of the stolen crypto?

The theft began around Oct. 29, when the Justice Department says North Korean military members known to the FBI as TraderTraitor stole more than $6 billion in virtual assets from Binance. After the theft, US agencies worked with virtual asset service providers, or VASPs—businesses that handle crypto transactions—to trace and intercept the funds.

When a VASP freezes assets, it blocks them from being moved through accounts or services it controls. That freeze is part of a broader tracing effort: investigators follow the stolen assets as they move between crypto addresses and services, then seek legal authority to take or restrain them. The Justice Department says the actors have continued laundering funds through crypto bridges, mixers and exchanges, and that efforts to trace and recover the assets remain underway.

Who is linked to the Binance theft?

The FBI attributes the theft to TraderTraitor, which the Justice Department describes as members of the North Korean military. The total amount frozen so far—more than $2.7 billion—reflects combined US government and private-sector efforts. The court-authorized amount, above $1.2 billion, is a portion of those frozen funds; the department has not said that the full amount has been transferred to government custody.

The distinction matters to Binance users and anyone with a claim to the assets. A freeze can prevent further movement while investigators work, but it does not by itself decide who ultimately owns the funds or whether they will be returned.

What does the court authorization change?

The authorization gives the government legal grounds to seek seizure or restraint of the specified assets. It does not, on its own, settle ownership. The Associated Press reports that the government typically files a forfeiture claim with a judge, who then determines whether anyone has a legitimate claim to recovered funds. The Justice Department’s announcement does not say that a final forfeiture decision has been made for this crypto.

What happens next in the recovery effort?

Authorities say tracing, freezing, seizure and forfeiture work is continuing as the stolen assets move through crypto services. The immediate change is legal authority covering more than $1.2 billion of funds already included in the wider freeze effort. The next steps to watch are further recovery actions and any forfeiture proceedings that determine whether, and to whom, recovered assets can be returned.
