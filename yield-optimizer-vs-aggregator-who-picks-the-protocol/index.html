Yield optimizer vs aggregator: who picks the protocol?

Yield optimizer vs yield aggregator: an optimizer compounds rewards in one strategy; an aggregator allocates across protocols, often choosing where your deposit goes.

The Crypto Front Page DeskNov 28, 2026 · 03:32 UTC3 min read

Choose a yield optimizer when you want to select the pool and have a strategy compound its rewards; choose a yield aggregator when you want the product to allocate your deposit across protocols. The terms describe different jobs, though many products combine them, so the useful question is who decides where your funds go.

An optimizer compounds inside a chosen strategy; an aggregator allocates across strategies

A yield optimizer takes a deposit placed in a chosen opportunity, then harvests rewards and reinvests them to grow that position. The steps are: the strategy puts the deposit to work, rewards accrue, the strategy collects them, and it converts and redeposits them. That auto-compounding can increase the amount working in the same strategy without asking you to harvest manually.

A yield aggregator takes a broader role: it allocates a deposit among strategies or protocols. It can spread funds across several opportunities, or shift allocations as rates and other conditions change. Like a manager assigning money to different baskets, it decides how much goes where; an optimizer’s central job is to tend the chosen basket.

The distinction shows up in the number of strategies behind one deposit. A Beefy app vault runs one active strategy at a time, which focuses the deposit on one yield opportunity. That strategy harvests and reinvests rewards. In this sense, the Beefy app is mainly an optimizer: it suits someone who wants to choose a specific vault and have its strategy handle compounding.

Yearn V3 vaults show how the jobs can overlap. A vault can allocate one deposit across multiple strategies, while those strategies work to generate yield and can compound returns. So calling a product an aggregator does not mean it lacks optimization; it describes the allocation layer, while compounding describes what happens within a strategy.

Who picks the protocol determines which product fits

For a yield optimizer, you choose the pool or vault that points to a strategy. The strategy handles the recurring work of harvesting and reinvesting rewards there. What earns the extra yield is the compounding process: rewards are put back to work in the same chosen opportunity, adding to the position over time.

For an aggregator, the product’s allocation logic or managers decide how a vault’s deposit is distributed among its available strategies. That can mean moving funds from one protocol to another without a new transaction from you, because the allocation happens within the vault. What may add yield is the choice or mix of opportunities; the product is selecting where capital works, not only compounding rewards from one position.

Before depositing, look at the vault’s strategy details and ask three things: who chooses the protocol, how many strategies can hold the deposit, and what process is meant to earn the additional yield? A single strategy that reinvests harvests points to an optimizer. A vault that allocates across strategies points to an aggregator, even when those strategies also compound. The Beefy app is a straightforward optimizer example; Yearn V3 illustrates allocation combined with strategy-level yield generation.

Choose an optimizer when you want to pick the exact pool and have one strategy compound its rewards. Choose an aggregator when you want the product to pick how your deposit is allocated across protocols. The key difference is who chooses where the funds go; check the vault’s current strategy setup to see which job it performs.
