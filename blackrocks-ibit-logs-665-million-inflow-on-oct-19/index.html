BlackRock’s IBIT Logs $665 Million Inflow on Oct. 19

BlackRock’s IBIT took in $665.16 million on Oct. 19, its biggest daily inflow since October 2025, lifting U.S. spot bitcoin ETFs to a $526.6 million net gain.

The Crypto Front Page DeskOct 20, 2026 · 09:43 UTC3 min read

BlackRock’s iShares Bitcoin Trust (IBIT) recorded $665.16 million in net inflows on Monday, Oct. 19, its largest one-day intake since Oct. 7, 2025, and the main reason U.S. spot bitcoin ETFs ended the day with net inflows. The Block, citing SoSoValue data, reported the figures; its report put total net inflows across the U.S. funds at $526.6 million.

A spot bitcoin ETF gives investors exposure to bitcoin through shares traded on an exchange. When money flows into a fund, the fund creates shares and acquires more bitcoin; when money flows out, shares are redeemed and exposure is reduced. The daily flow figure records the net result of those creations and redemptions, rather than simply tracking the number of shares traded between investors.

Why did IBIT’s inflow exceed the total for all funds?

Because other funds had outflows that offset part of IBIT’s intake. CryptoETF’s daily flow data records IBIT at $665.2 million on Oct. 19, while Fidelity’s FBTC had $83.6 million in outflows and Grayscale’s GBTC had $54.9 million in outflows. The other tracked funds reported no net creations or redemptions that day. In the dashboard’s rounded figures, the 12 U.S. spot bitcoin ETFs together gained $526.7 million.

The difference between IBIT’s $665.2 million and the roughly $526.6 million combined total reflects those withdrawals from other products. In practical terms, the daily net figure works like a running balance: IBIT’s large addition was reduced by money leaving FBTC and GBTC.

What does the record say about demand?

The inflow shows that IBIT’s share creations outpaced redemptions by a substantial amount that day. It does not, by itself, explain why investors added exposure or establish a lasting trend. The Block reported that the exact driver was unclear; an analyst it cited said investors might be positioning for a potential catch-up trade in bitcoin.

The distinction between a fund-level inflow and the wider market matters. IBIT was the only one of the 12 funds with a net inflow on Oct. 19, according to CryptoETF’s flow breakdown, even as its intake was large enough to leave the whole group positive after other funds’ outflows.

What should readers watch next?

The next daily flow reports will show whether creations continue at IBIT and whether demand spreads to other funds. For Oct. 19, the confirmed change was a $665.16 million net inflow to IBIT and a positive net total for U.S. spot bitcoin ETFs. The figures describe fund flows; they do not identify the investors or establish what they will do next.

The Block reported IBIT’s $665.16 million inflow and the comparison with October 2025. CryptoETF’s daily flow data breaks down the Oct. 19 net figures by fund and explains how creations and redemptions are reflected in ETF flows.
