How to Respond After a Fake Site Drains Your Wallet

My wallet was drained after approving a fake site. Find out whether you approved a token allowance or exposed your seed, then protect what remains and report the theft.

The Crypto Front Page DeskNov 28, 2026 · 04:46 UTC4 min read

Stop interacting with the fake site, then find out whether you approved a token allowance or exposed your seed phrase. The fix depends on what the attacker gained: an allowance gives access to certain tokens, while a seed phrase gives control of the whole wallet.

How can I tell what the fake site got?

Start with the wallet activity and the information you entered or signed. In an ERC-20 approval, your wallet sets how much a spender can take from that token balance using transferFrom. An EIP-2612 permit can grant an allowance through an off-chain signature, so the approval may appear in your activity only after someone submits it. Permit2 uses signatures to authorize token transfers, too.

A fake USDC bridge may ask you to approve a token contract so it can move USDC from one blockchain to another. That cross-chain transfer is the feature that makes choosing a USDC bridge route a useful example of why an approval prompt matters: the spender named in the prompt gets permission to move tokens. A malicious site can substitute its own spender.

Look at the transaction in the wallet’s activity or on the relevant chain’s block explorer. An approval or permit points to a spender and a token; a transfer shows assets leaving. If you typed your seed phrase into the site, treat the wallet as fully compromised even if the history shows only one token moving. Anyone with the phrase can control every asset and account derived from it.

  • The site URL and the time you visited: these help identify the phishing page and match its activity to transactions.
  • The approval or signature prompt, if you saved a screenshot: it may show the token, spender, or requested permissions.
  • Your wallet address and transaction hashes: they let investigators and support teams trace the theft.
  • Whether you entered your seed phrase: this determines whether revoking an allowance is enough.

What should I do to protect what remains?

For an approval drain, revoke the open allowance for the affected token and spender. Use Revoke.cash or Etherscan’s Token Approval Checker on the correct EVM chain, connect the wallet, identify the suspicious allowance, and set it to zero. Revocation is an on-chain transaction and costs gas. It stops future spending through that allowance; it cannot reverse tokens already taken.

For a leaked seed phrase, create a new wallet on a clean device and move any remaining assets there immediately. Do not send gas to the compromised wallet to pay for a revoke: sweeper bots may take newly deposited gas before your transaction can be confirmed. Revoking is for an exposed approval, not a stolen seed. Never reuse the leaked phrase in the new wallet.

Take this short case: you approved a site, and USDC left your wallet. First, open the transaction and note the token and spender; now you know which allowance to inspect. Next, use the checker and submit a zero-allowance transaction; once it confirms, that spender cannot use that allowance again. Then save the theft transaction hash and report it. If you discover you entered your seed instead, skip the revoke step and move the remaining assets to a fresh wallet; the old wallet remains compromised.

If neither an approval nor a seed leak explains the loss, stop signing prompts and inspect recent transactions and connected apps from a clean device. Save addresses, timestamps, transaction hashes, and screenshots. Report the theft to Chainabuse and the FBI’s IC3; include the hashes, token amounts, dates, and wallet addresses. If USDC was taken, report the addresses and transactions to Circle as well. Circle can blacklist addresses on the USDC contract, but that does not reverse a transfer or ensure funds are returned.

Related

Can stolen crypto be recovered?

Usually, a confirmed blockchain transfer cannot be reversed by the sender. Report it promptly with transaction details; recovery depends on whether authorities, an exchange, or the token issuer can identify or freeze funds. Be wary of anyone demanding an upfront fee to recover crypto.

Does a hardware wallet protect against a malicious approval?

A hardware wallet protects the seed from being exposed to a website, but it can still sign a malicious approval. Read the token, spender, and amount on the device before confirming. Revoke any approval you do not recognize, and watch the wallet for further transactions.
