How to bridge crypto during a sharp market move

During a sharp move, check the route, final amount, fees, settlement path and destination gas before bridging; urgency can make a slow or thin route costly.

The Crypto Front Page DeskOct 17, 2026 · 07:12 UTC4 min read

To bridge crypto during a sharp market move, check the route, the amount expected to arrive and how settlement works before signing. A bridge moves assets or messages between separate blockchains; it does not make the chains share one ledger. The transfer usually involves a transaction on the source chain, a process that verifies or routes it, and a transaction on the destination chain.

How does a crypto bridge move assets?

First, you choose the source and destination networks, token and amount. The bridge checks whether it supports that combination and displays a route and estimate. Depending on its design, it may lock the token on the source chain and release or mint a corresponding asset on the destination, or use liquidity already available there to pay out.

Next, you approve the token if needed and submit the source transaction. The bridge waits for the source transaction to reach the required confirmation or finality point, then a contract, validator set or relayer verifies the transfer and triggers the destination step. The destination transaction can take additional time and may require gas paid in that chain’s native token. For more detail on comparing route cost with speed, see Biswap’s guide to cross-chain swap costs and speed.

During a volatile move, these stages matter because the displayed estimate can change before the transfer completes. Some routes transfer the same token across chains; others include a swap, so the amount delivered can also depend on exchange prices and liquidity. A bridge receipt or source-chain confirmation does not always mean the destination transaction has finished.

What should I compare before bridging?

Compare the amount that should arrive, the total fees and the route’s expected completion time. The cheapest route can be a poor fit if it settles slowly or has little destination liquidity; the fastest route may charge more or deliver a less favorable swap. Check what the quote includes, since source-chain gas, bridge fees, destination gas and swap costs may appear as separate deductions.

  • Route: Confirm the exact source and destination networks, token and recipient address.
  • Net amount: Look at the estimated destination balance after fees and any included swap.
  • Settlement: Check whether the estimate covers both the source confirmation and destination completion.
  • Destination gas: Make sure you will have the chain’s required gas token to use or move the funds after arrival.

On a busy or fast-moving market, quotes can expire, fees can rise and thin liquidity can widen the difference between the displayed amount and the amount received. Recheck the route just before signing. If the bridge requires a separate token approval, verify that it is for the intended asset and contract. Avoid sending funds to an address or network you have not checked; transfers across chains can be difficult or impossible to reverse.

How do I handle a transfer that is still pending?

Use the transaction status and source-chain transaction ID to see which stage has completed. If the source transaction is still pending, the transfer has not yet reached the bridge’s verification step. If it is confirmed but the destination transaction is pending, check the bridge’s status page and instructions before attempting another transfer. Sending a second transaction can create a second transfer rather than speed up the first.

Keep enough of the source chain’s native token to pay for the initial transaction, and enough destination gas to act once the assets arrive. If speed is essential, compare the complete route again and decide whether the faster option’s fee and expected amount are acceptable before submitting. A bridge’s estimate is not a guarantee of arrival time or final value, especially when a route includes a swap.

The practical sequence is simple: confirm that bridging is necessary, compare the net amount and settlement path, check both networks and gas requirements, then sign only after the quote still matches your needs. During a sharp move, the route and its fees can change quickly; watch the transfer status through destination completion and verify the received token and balance before using the funds.
