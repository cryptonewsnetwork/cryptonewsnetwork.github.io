EU proposal targets Russia-linked crypto and payment networks

The European Commission has proposed adding crypto and payment networks to Russia sanctions, with rules reaching third countries accused of helping sanctions evasion.

The Crypto Front Page DeskNov 24, 2026 · 23:53 UTC3 min read

The European Commission’s proposed 22nd sanctions package would extend financial restrictions through transaction bans on Russian banks and measures against crypto and payment networks, President Ursula von der Leyen said on Nov. 23. The Commission’s announcement says the measures would focus on Russia and third countries that help it evade sanctions.

What would the proposal change for crypto?

It would give crypto and payment networks a place in the EU’s next sanctions push, but the Commission’s statement does not name specific platforms or describe the exact restrictions each would face. Von der Leyen said the package would crack down on networks that help Russia bypass existing sanctions. The Commission has also launched a Crypto Sanctions Task Force to support that work and coordinate with international partners.

The sequence is consequential: the Commission proposes the measures, EU governments consider them, and any adopted restrictions would then apply under the package’s rules. At this proposal stage, the public announcement describes the target and purpose, but not a list of affected crypto services or a detailed compliance process.

How do the bank measures connect to payments?

The proposal would extend transaction bans to about 60 Russian banks and remove all 60 from SWIFT, the messaging system banks use for international payments. SWIFT acts like a switchboard for payment instructions: excluding a bank disrupts its access to that messaging network, while the transaction ban separately restricts dealings with it. The two measures address different parts of cross-border finance.

The Associated Press report says the EU’s proposed crypto measures could also reach countries helping Russia evade sanctions. Neither source identifies those countries or crypto platforms in this announcement.

When could the restrictions take effect?

They are proposals, not rules already in force. AP reports that all 27 EU member countries must endorse the package before it can take effect; that process can take weeks, and the measures may change. The Commission has not yet set out a final list of covered crypto services in the announcement.

The proposal also reaches beyond finance: it would bar Russian teams and athletes from competing under the Russian flag at sports events in the EU, restrict official Russian symbols, and curb financial flows from Russian sponsors to European sports and cultural associations. Those measures sit alongside the bank, energy and visa proposals in the same package.

What changed on Nov. 23 is that the Commission formally proposed adding crypto and payment networks to its Russia sanctions strategy and created a task force to support enforcement. The next things to watch are the member states’ approval process and the final text defining which services and transactions are covered.
