How attackers drained $6.3 billion from Binance

Attackers drained about $6.3 billion from Binance wallets on Oct. 29 by abusing transaction-signing systems, exposing how exchange controls can fail at scale.

The Crypto Front Page DeskNov 6, 2026 · 06:54 UTC3 min read

Hackers drained about $6.3 billion from Binance wallets on Oct. 29, in what blockchain firms and the exchange describe as the largest crypto theft on record. The transfers took place across multiple blockchains, with most of the stolen funds moved in about 20 minutes, according to Reuters’ account of the attack.

Binance said it initially suspected a compromised laptop, then found additional points of breach. It has not publicly detailed all the access points. The Associated Press report on the breach says blockchain firms suspect North Korea’s TraderTraitor group; Binance said it was working with law enforcement to identify the attackers. The previous crypto theft record cited in coverage was the $1.5 billion Bybit hack in 2025.

How did attackers get Binance wallets to send funds?

Investigators say the attackers compromised Binance’s transaction-signing infrastructure: the systems that authorize transfers from its hot wallets, which are used to process transactions. Reuters, citing blockchain analysis firm TRM Labs, reported that this access let attackers issue instructions that emptied the affected wallets. The evidence points to misuse of the system that approved transactions; Binance has not confirmed that private keys were stolen.

Think of it as getting a dispatch system to send a vault’s contents, rather than taking the vault key. Once transfers were authorized, the funds moved on public blockchains, where investigators could trace their destinations. The attack shows why securing the approval process matters alongside protecting wallet keys.

Why could some stolen crypto be frozen?

Some issuers can freeze their tokens at specific addresses, while Bitcoin and ether have no central issuer with that power. Reuters reported that stablecoins made up about half the stolen assets, and issuers including Tether and Circle froze affected funds. Binance said it had worked with partners to freeze roughly $3 billion.

A freeze can block further transfers of those tokens, but it does not automatically return them to Binance. Bitcoin and ether generally have to reach an exchange or broker willing to freeze them, or be recovered through law enforcement action. Investigators told Reuters that some of the remaining funds were being moved and converted after the theft.

What has Binance said it will do next?

Binance has promised to cover users’ losses and said it reserved more than $6.34 billion in company funds for that purpose, according to AP. It said it was beginning to recover assets for users. The exchange’s investigation and law enforcement work may clarify how attackers gained access and who was responsible.

What changed is the scale of the confirmed loss and the evidence that transaction-signing systems were compromised. The next things to watch are what Binance discloses about those systems, how much stolen crypto can be recovered, and whether authorities identify the attackers.
