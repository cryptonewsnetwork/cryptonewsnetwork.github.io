Consensys launches stack to coordinate settlement across ledgers

Consensys launched a stack to route transactions across institutional ledgers, coordinating settlement while assets stay native and institutions retain control.

The Crypto Front Page DeskNov 8, 2026 · 03:41 UTC3 min read

Consensys announced Sovereign Network Interoperability on November 2 in Mumbai, a technology stack that routes and coordinates transactions across independently operated institutional ledgers while leaving assets on their native networks. The company’s announcement says it is designed for banks, custodians, asset managers and financial market infrastructures that need to transact with counterparties on other ledgers.

How does the stack coordinate a transaction?

An institution first specifies what it wants to do, which counterparties it has approved and the settlement requirements. The software then selects an eligible route, coordinates execution on the participating ledgers and tracks the transaction through completion, failure or a need for intervention. It acts like a dispatcher: it directs the instruction to the right connections and follows its progress, while the ledgers record the assets and transaction activity.

Consensys says the stack’s core protocol is its Cross Ledger Interoperability Protocol, or CLIP. It uses cryptographic verification for cross-ledger state and execution. In the company’s description, this “bridgeless” design keeps assets on their native ledgers instead of moving them into bridge-issued representations. The announcement says the approach avoids additional trusted verification intermediaries; participating ledgers, verification software and operational controls still carry defined trust assumptions.

Which financial workflows and networks are included?

The stack is intended to coordinate four types of activity: posting collateral to an approved counterparty; delivering a tokenized security against payment on another ledger; coordinating the two legs of a foreign-exchange transaction; and settling tokenized deposits against fund units. For the last use case, Consensys describes treasurers moving idle cash into tokenized money market funds and back.

The Block’s report also says EVM-based networks are available now, while Canton connectivity is planned. Consensys says the stack will also support the Ethereum Economic Zone standard, the Inter-Blockchain Communication protocol and other interoperability protocols. Institutions can use their existing infrastructure, connecting supported networks through software upgrades, contracts or adapters, according to the company.

What changes for institutions using separate ledgers?

The product packages routing, execution coordination and transaction tracking with maintained adapters and software releases. Consensys says customers can operate it themselves, use a financial market infrastructure or choose Consensys-managed services. Reusable integrations are intended to reduce bespoke work when adding a counterparty or workflow; the company also offers compatibility testing, upgrades, monitoring and incident handling.

The announcement adds a supported coordination layer between financial networks without requiring institutions to replace their ledgers or move assets onto another chain. EVM-based connections are available now; the next thing to watch is how the planned Canton connection and supported protocols are brought into live workflows.
