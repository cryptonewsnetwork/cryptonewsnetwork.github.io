Coinbase’s National Trust Charter Takes Effect

Coinbase’s national trust company took effect under OCC oversight, bringing crypto custody into a federal framework while keeping the charter’s scope narrow.

The Crypto Front Page DeskNov 10, 2026 · 00:14 UTC3 min read

Coinbase’s national trust charter took effect on Nov. 6, placing its planned institutional crypto custody business under federal oversight by the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC). Coinbase said the OCC gave final approval to Coinbase National Trust Company; the regulator’s charter filing record lists Nov. 6 as its consummation and effective date.

The sequence began with Coinbase’s application on Oct. 3, 2025. The OCC granted preliminary conditional approval on April 2, 2026, subject to requirements the new bank had to meet before opening. The agency’s April decision describes the proposed institution as a national trust bank, not a general-purpose bank.

What will Coinbase National Trust Company do?

It is designed to hold and safeguard digital assets for customers, chiefly institutional clients, in a fiduciary capacity. That means the trust company holds assets on a customer’s behalf and must meet duties and standards of conduct attached to that role.

The OCC’s decision also describes related services for custody customers: accepting, holding and transferring fiat currency and digital assets in their custody accounts. Fiat held in custody would sit in “for benefit of” accounts at third-party banks. The trust company may also connect its custody clients to certain Coinbase affiliate services, including staking, prime trading and prime financing, but the OCC said the trust company itself would not provide those underlying services.

How does the charter differ from a full-service bank?

The charter authorizes trust company operations and related activities, not ordinary deposit-taking and commercial lending. Coinbase National Trust Company will not be an insured depository institution, according to the OCC’s decision. In practical terms, it is closer to a secure vault with regulated services around moving and managing customers’ assets than to a bank where deposits fund loans.

The OCC also said Coinbase’s application did not propose issuing stablecoins. While the charter’s scope is limited, the regulator’s April decision set capital and liquidity requirements for the new institution: at least $60 million in Tier 1 capital, with a specified portion held in eligible liquid assets, and liquid assets equal to 180 days of operating expenses. Those conditions apply during its first three years of operation.

What changes for customers now?

The change is the federal charter and oversight framework. Coinbase’s announcement says it can expand custody under that framework, but it does not give a date for when the trust company will begin operating or when custody accounts will move. The OCC decision said Coinbase planned to migrate its existing custody business from its New York-chartered trust company over a three-year period.

The next step to watch is the trust company’s operational launch and any customer migration details Coinbase provides. For now, the OCC record confirms the charter is effective; the service transition remains to come.
