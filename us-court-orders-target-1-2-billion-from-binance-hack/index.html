US court orders target $1.2 billion from Binance hack

The Justice Department says court orders now cover more than $1.2 billion of Binance hack proceeds, as investigators and crypto firms trace and freeze funds.

The Crypto Front Page DeskNov 20, 2026 · 14:58 UTC3 min read

U.S. authorities are tracing and freezing cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, then using court orders to seek control of some of it: the Justice Department says more than $1.2 billion is now subject to authorization to seize or restrain. In a November 12 announcement, the department said the funds are part of a theft of more than $6 billion that the FBI attributes to North Korean actors called TraderTraitor.

How did authorities move from a hack to a court order?

The FBI says the theft took place on or about October 29, 2026. After the assets left Binance, investigators worked with virtual asset service providers, or VASPs, to identify and interdict them. These providers include businesses that handle cryptocurrency transfers or services; the FBI’s alert also names DeFi services, instant exchanges and blockchain infrastructure operators among those it asked to block transactions linked to the stolen funds.

The process has several steps. Investigators trace transfers from addresses associated with the theft. They share identified addresses with industry partners, which can block transactions or freeze assets they control. Authorities then seek court authorization to seize or restrain assets. The FBI says the attackers have continued laundering funds through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges, so tracing and blocking remain ongoing. In its October 31 alert, the FBI said it had observed the actors converting stolen assets primarily into Ether and expected further laundering through DeFi and instant swap services.

Who is holding the frozen funds, and who may claim them?

The Justice Department said U.S. government and private sector efforts had frozen more than $2.7 billion of the stolen assets. More than $1.2 billion of that total has court authorization to be seized or restrained. Those figures describe different stages: the broader amount frozen by government and private partners, and the portion covered by court authorization. The announcement does not say that all $1.2 billion has been finally forfeited or returned to customers.

The next legal step can be a forfeiture claim filed with a judge. The Associated Press reports that a judge would determine who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds. That means the seizure effort and any decision about who ultimately receives assets are separate parts of the process.

What changes now, and what remains to watch?

The court authorization gives investigators a legal route to pursue part of the frozen assets, while the larger tracing effort continues across services and blockchains. For Binance users, it is a recovery step, but the public announcements do not establish how much will ultimately be returned or when. The next developments to watch are further freezes or seizures, court forfeiture decisions, and whether investigators or service providers identify additional addresses used to move the stolen funds.
