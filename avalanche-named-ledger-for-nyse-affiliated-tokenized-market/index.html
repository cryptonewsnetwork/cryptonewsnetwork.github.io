Avalanche named ledger for NYSE-affiliated tokenized market

Ava Labs says Avalanche will provide the ledger for clearing trades on an NYSE-affiliated tokenized securities venue designed to trade and settle around the clock.

The Crypto Front Page DeskNov 10, 2026 · 12:00 UTC3 min read

Ava Labs says Avalanche will provide the core ledger for the clearing firm of an NYSE-affiliated digital trading venue, a planned step toward recording and settling tokenized securities trades onchain around the clock. The Ava Labs announcement, issued November 9, says the platform’s 24/7 trading, clearing and settlement design is subject to regulatory approvals.

What job will Avalanche do in the venue?

Avalanche will provide ledger technology to the platform’s clearing firm. In plain terms, the ledger is the shared transaction book: it records trades and supports their settlement. Ava Labs chief executive Emin Gün Sirer described the goal as “atomic settlement,” where the trade and its payment are completed together. The announcement does not specify the technical steps or assets used to fund each trade.

The planned venue is an alternative trading system affiliated with the New York Stock Exchange. NYSE Head of Digital Issuance Dennis Lee said in the announcement that equities on the platform may be listed on different blockchains, while Avalanche would serve the separate function of recording and settling trades. That distinction means the securities’ blockchains and the settlement ledger do not have to be the same.

How would a trade move through the system?

First, a subscriber would place a trade in the digital venue. The platform would match buyers and sellers; the announcement identifies Avalanche as the ledger technology supporting the clearing firm, but does not detail the matching process. Then the clearing firm would use the ledger to record and settle the transaction. The stated design aims to make those services available 24 hours a day, seven days a week, subject to regulatory approval.

The Block’s report on the selection says NYSE had tested Avalanche for about a year. It also reports that the venue is intended to support equities issued across multiple blockchains. Neither the announcement nor the report says the new platform is operating now.

What still has to happen before it can launch?

Regulatory approvals remain a condition for the platform. The November 9 announcement did not give a launch date or name the specific securities that would trade there. It also did not say which assets the venue would accept for payment or describe how subscriber access would be controlled, though Lee said control over participation was among the ledger’s requirements.

The confirmed change is the selection of Avalanche for the clearing firm’s core ledger on a planned NYSE-affiliated tokenized securities venue. The next concrete milestones to watch are regulatory approvals and further details about how the venue will connect trade matching, the clearing firm and settlement across the supported blockchains.
