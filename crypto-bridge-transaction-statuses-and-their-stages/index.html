Crypto Bridge Transaction Statuses and Their Stages

Crypto bridge transaction statuses show whether a deposit awaits source confirmation, is being relayed between chains, or has been minted on the destination chain.

The Crypto Front Page DeskNov 26, 2026 · 16:44 UTC4 min read

An Ethereum-to-Sui deposit moves through source confirmation, bridge processing, and destination minting; its status tells you which step is underway.

A bridge transfer has two chain actions: an asset is locked on Ethereum, then a corresponding asset is minted on Sui after the cross-chain message is handled. Status labels name parts of that sequence, though each interface may use different wording. Suibridge follows this lock-and-mint process for supported Ethereum-to-Sui transfers. Its transfer-state representation is useful background on how an app can show those steps.

What do crypto bridge transaction statuses mean?

Common bridge statuses indicate whether the source deposit is waiting for confirmation, the bridge is relaying its message, or the destination asset has been minted.

Submitted or pending usually means the source transaction has been sent. It may still be waiting to be included in an Ethereum block or to receive the confirmations the bridge requires. Find the Ethereum transaction hash in the transfer interface and look it up on an Ethereum explorer. The record shows whether the transaction was included and its confirmation progress; a submitted label by itself only describes the interface’s current view.

Processing, relaying, or a similar label generally means the source deposit has been confirmed and the bridge is handling the cross-chain message. The Sui mint may not be visible yet. This is the middle step: Ethereum has recorded the deposit, but the destination action is still outstanding.

Completed or received usually means the destination action has occurred. Look for the Sui transaction digest, then inspect it on a Sui explorer. Confirm that the transaction succeeded and that the receiving address is your Sui wallet address. The digest identifies Sui activity; the Ethereum transaction hash identifies Ethereum activity. They are separate records for separate parts of one transfer.

Failed, delayed, or stuck calls for checking the relevant chain record and the bridge’s own status guidance. A failed Ethereum deposit and a delayed destination mint point to different steps in the process. A label alone cannot identify the cause, so compare the Ethereum hash or Sui digest with the stage shown by the interface before deciding what to do next.

Here is an illustrative transfer: a person sends a supported asset from Ethereum to their Sui wallet. Before the transfer, the asset is on Ethereum and the Sui wallet does not yet show the bridged asset. After the Ethereum deposit is confirmed and the bridge processes the message, a corresponding asset is minted on Sui for the receiving address. The destination asset represents the completed bridge action; the Ethereum hash and Sui digest remain records on their own chains.

What does pending mean on a crypto bridge?

Pending means the transfer has been started but the interface has not yet moved it to a later stage. Open the Ethereum transaction hash first: if the deposit is not included in a block, it is still awaiting source-chain inclusion. If it is included, check its confirmation progress and the bridge’s stated status. Once the source step is confirmed, a processing or relaying label may appear while the destination mint is underway.

Why is my bridged crypto not showing in my wallet?

A missing asset can mean the destination mint has not happened yet, or that you are checking the wrong address or chain record. Find the Sui transaction digest and verify that the transaction succeeded and names the address for your Sui wallet. If there is no digest, inspect the Ethereum hash and the bridge status to see whether the transfer is still at the source or relay stage. Use the bridge’s guidance to investigate a failed or delayed transfer.
