DOJ files to forfeit $1.1 billion in Binance-linked USDC

The Justice Department filed to forfeit $1.1 billion in USDC tied to Binance’s October theft, putting the frozen tokens on a path toward a court decision.

The Crypto Front Page DeskNov 27, 2026 · 21:32 UTC3 min read

The Justice Department has filed a civil case to forfeit more than $1.1 billion in USDC linked to the October theft from Binance, a step that could move the frozen funds into U.S. custody if a judge approves. The department’s Nov. 23 announcement says the filing follows an earlier court order restraining the assets.

What is the government asking the court to forfeit?

The property named in the complaint is USDC, a dollar-pegged stablecoin issued by Circle. According to the Justice Department, the tokens are proceeds of the Binance theft, which investigators say occurred on or about Oct. 29, 2026, and involved about $6 billion in cryptocurrency.

The filing identifies USDC held at dozens of addresses across multiple blockchain networks. Federal investigators attribute the theft to North Korean military hackers; the complaint says the FBI, Secret Service and IRS Criminal Investigation assess that the attack was carried out by the Lazarus Group, also known as APT38.

A civil forfeiture case is brought against the property itself. The government’s filing asks the court to determine whether the USDC can legally be forfeited; it does not, by itself, complete the recovery or establish a final ruling.

How were the tokens stopped from moving?

The complaint says a federal court issued a temporary restraining order on Nov. 12, and the order was served on Circle the same day. Circle can block designated addresses from sending or receiving USDC on the blockchains where it issues the token. That works like putting a lock on identified accounts: the tokens remain recorded at their addresses, but those addresses cannot use them normally.

The government says it cannot directly transfer the restrained USDC because the addresses remain under the hackers’ control. Circle has told the government it cannot move those tokens either. Instead, Circle has agreed to issue an equivalent amount of new USDC and transfer it to the United States if the court issues a forfeiture order, according to the forfeiture complaint filed in the District of Columbia.

What happens to the USDC now?

A judge must consider the forfeiture claim before the substitute tokens can be transferred to the government. The filing is therefore a legal step toward recovery, rather than confirmation that the $1.1 billion has already been returned to Binance or its customers.

The Justice Department says U.S. government and private-sector efforts had frozen more than $2.7 billion connected to the theft, and that the government had obtained court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion. It also said more than $2.9 billion had been frozen, seized or returned from multiple sources since the theft. Those totals describe different stages and actions; the new forfeiture case concerns the specified USDC property.

The next development to watch is the court’s decision on the forfeiture request. Until then, the addresses remain blocked and Circle’s proposed replacement USDC depends on a court order.
