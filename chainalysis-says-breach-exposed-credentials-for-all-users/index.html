Chainalysis says breach exposed credentials for all users

Chainalysis says an attacker exposed names, usernames, password hashes and live tokens for all users, prompting account resets and a staged shutdown of its tools.

The Crypto Front Page DeskNov 12, 2026 · 10:39 UTC3 min read

Chainalysis said on Nov. 4 that an attacker had exposed names, usernames, password hashes and live tokens for all its users, expanding the scope of a breach first disclosed five days earlier. The company’s incident updates show how its account changed as the investigation progressed: the first notice said there was no evidence credentials or session tokens had been accessed; the later update confirmed the exposure.

What information did the attacker expose?

Chainalysis said the exposed records included names, usernames, password hashes and live tokens for all users. A password hash is a transformed record used to check a password without storing it in plain text; it is not itself the original password. The company has not said how the passwords were hashed or what kind of tokens were exposed. The Record reported that active authentication tokens can allow account access without a password, but the precise risk depends on the token type and whether it remains valid.

The company also reported a separate exposure: on Nov. 4, it said an “on-path attack” had exposed passwords for a subset of customers. In that kind of attack, an intruder intercepts communications between a user and a service. Chainalysis said it had contained and fixed that attack vector, and locked affected accounts as a precaution. It did not say whether this interception was how the broader exposure of hashes and tokens occurred.

What did the investigation establish about the breach?

Chainalysis said it detected an external actor using “offensive agentic techniques” to gain unauthorized access to company systems and internal tools. It has not explained the techniques or detailed how the attacker moved through those systems. In its Nov. 4 update, the company said it had no evidence that investigation data had been accessed or its blockchain attribution data manipulated. The Record also reported that some customer accounts on Chainalysis’ Know Your Transaction service appeared to have been accessed without authorization.

How did the breach affect Chainalysis services?

Chainalysis took several products offline while it investigated and responded to abnormal activity. Its updates say Reactor and Data Solutions were taken offline on Nov. 2; on Nov. 4, the company also took down its KYT interface and API, other address-screening products and its login page. It required users to reset passwords and rotate API keys where applicable, and asked customers to provide trusted IP addresses to restore access.

The company said Reactor, KYT and Address Screening were back online by Nov. 7, with enhanced security measures, and Data Solutions returned on Nov. 10. The confirmed change is that Chainalysis’s initial account of a limited exposure broadened to include hashes and live tokens across all users, followed by a staged service restoration. Its updates do not yet specify the token type or provide a detailed breakdown of accessed records by customer.
