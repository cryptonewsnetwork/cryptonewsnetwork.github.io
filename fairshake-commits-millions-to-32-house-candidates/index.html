Fairshake commits millions to 32 House candidates

Fairshake will spend in 32 House races, backing lawmakers who supported crypto market rules as the stalled CLARITY Act leaves the industry seeking a next step.

The Crypto Front Page DeskOct 6, 20263 min read

Fairshake will put campaign spending behind 32 House incumbents who supported crypto market rules, the group announced as the industry’s CLARITY Act stalled in the Senate. The bipartisan slate includes 19 Republicans and 13 Democrats, according to the Associated Press report on Fairshake’s midterm plan.

How much is Fairshake committing to each race?

Fairshake has named six lawmakers for $1 million each: Democrats Janelle Bynum of Oregon, Steven Horsford of Nevada and Derek Tran of California, and Republicans French Hill of Arkansas, Bryan Steil of Wisconsin and Bill Huizenga of Michigan. The group has not disclosed how much it plans to spend on the other 26 candidates, Roll Call reported.

That means the public figure is a floor, not a total for the House slate: the six announced allocations add up to $6 million, while the rest remain unspecified. Fairshake’s announced plan is campaign spending to support candidates; the reports do not give a dollar amount for each of the other races.

Why are the candidates on the list?

The common link is their vote for the CLARITY Act, which passed the House in July 2025. The bill would set rules for digital-asset markets and divide oversight between federal regulators. The Senate did not reach agreement on ethics safeguards, including concerns about public officials’ personal crypto ventures, and blocked the measure on a procedural vote in September.

Fairshake says it backs candidates in both parties based on their support for crypto. In this plan, that means the group has taken the House vote as a signal for where to direct election spending. The sequence is straightforward: lawmakers backed the bill, the Senate effort stalled, and Fairshake announced support for those lawmakers’ reelection campaigns.

What does the stalled bill mean for Fairshake’s next moves?

The spending gives the industry a way to keep its policy priority in view as voters choose the next Congress, but it does not restart the bill or guarantee another vote. Fairshake said it is emphasizing its bipartisan slate as it looks to the next Congress to revive legislation, according to AP.

The House investment sits alongside Fairshake’s larger Senate commitment: the group said in September it planned to spend nearly $30 million against Democratic candidate Sherrod Brown in Ohio. AP reported that Fairshake and its affiliated PACs had about $120 million in cash at the end of August; Roll Call reported $108 million for Fairshake PAC itself. Those figures cover different entities.

What changed is that Fairshake has named 32 House races for support, with at least $6 million specified. The next markers are the spending disclosures for the other candidates and whether Congress takes up digital-asset market rules again after the midterms.
