Move Funds Off a Closing Chain Before Exits Narrow

To move funds off a blockchain network that is shutting down, unwind positions, bridge tokens to a live chain, and confirm they arrive before cutoff.

The Crypto Front Page DeskNov 28, 2026 · 07:03 UTC5 min read

Read the shutdown notice first, because its cutoff date and supported exits determine how much time you have to move funds. To move funds off a blockchain network that is shutting down, unwind positions into wallet tokens, bridge them to a live chain, then confirm they appear at your destination address.

Jumper bridge can find routes through supported bridges and swaps when you want to reach a live destination quickly. Its quotes may use providers such as Across or Stargate; the chain’s own bridge is another route and may remain available longer. Third-party routes can close before the announced shutdown as liquidity providers leave, so compare the options early.

Move wallet assets before the cutoff

  1. Note the cutoff and supported exits. Read the project’s shutdown notice for the last date to transact, any bridge or exchange instructions, and whether deposits or withdrawals will stop earlier. For rollups, L2BEAT’s risk and exit-window pages list forced-exit paths; check the entry for your specific network.
  2. Unwind positions into plain tokens. Withdraw liquidity-provider (LP) funds, unstake, and repay or close lending positions before bridging. Only tokens available in your wallet can be sent through a bridge. Leave time for any position-specific withdrawal or claim process, and keep the chain’s native gas token for transactions.
  3. Choose a live destination. Pick a chain where you can access the assets after they arrive, and make sure you have a wallet address there. The route might convert a token along the way, so read the quote’s assets, fees, and destination before approving it.
  4. Quote the route and send a small amount. Set the sunsetting network as the source in Jumper bridge, choose the live destination, and review the route. Bridging requires the source chain’s native gas token in your wallet, including for a final transfer of that token. Send a small amount first when the route and balance allow; confirm it arrives before moving the rest.
  5. Bridge the remaining tokens, gas token last. Keep enough native token to pay for every remaining approval and bridge transaction. Spending it first can strand other wallet assets because you may be unable to submit the transactions that move them. Transfer the gas token only after the other assets are moving or complete.
  6. Use another supported exit when no route appears. Check the chain’s canonical bridge and the project’s notice. A native bridge can be slower, but may stay open after third-party routes disappear. An exchange deposit is an option only when that exchange still supports deposits for that exact network and token. Confirm the network and deposit details before sending.
  7. Confirm the destination balance. Open the wallet on the live chain and verify each expected token is visible at the destination address. A source-side transaction marked complete is not the final check; the funds should be present on the destination network.

For instance, a holder with tokens on Arbitrum who wants them on Ethereum can first close lending and staking positions, then quote the remaining wallet tokens. If a route through Across is available, the holder can send a small transfer, check it on Ethereum, and move the rest while retaining Arbitrum gas for the final transaction. If that route disappears, the holder should check Arbitrum’s canonical bridge and its withdrawal timing.

What happens to tokens left on a chain after it shuts down?

They are not automatically transferred to another network. Their accessibility depends on the chain’s operating state, wallet and node access, and any remaining bridge or exchange support. Follow the shutdown notice and arrange an exit before the cutoff; after services stop, options may be limited or unavailable.

Can I still withdraw through the canonical bridge after the sequencer stops?

It depends on the rollup’s design and the shutdown plan. Some rollups list a forced-exit path, but its steps and timing vary. Check the network’s L2BEAT entry and the team’s notice before relying on that route. A withdrawal may involve a delay and a later claim transaction.

The answer changes when the team updates its cutoff, closes a bridge, or stops supporting an exchange deposit. Recheck the notice and available exit paths as those dates approach; near shutdown, the chain’s own bridge may be the remaining route.

Questions to check before moving assets

Are tokens left behind lost after the shutdown?

Not necessarily, but a token balance on the old chain does not move by itself. Access depends on whether the chain’s data and transaction tools remain usable and whether an exit route still operates. Save the notice and transaction records, and check the project’s instructions promptly.

How do I exit an NFT from a chain that is closing?

Check whether the NFT collection and destination chain have a supported bridge or migration process; a token route may not support NFTs. Follow the collection’s official instructions, and test with a low-value item only if the route supports that transfer.

Is the move taxable?

Tax treatment depends on your jurisdiction and the transactions involved, including swaps or closing positions. Keep transaction hashes, timestamps, token amounts, and records of any swaps. Ask a qualified tax adviser how to report them under local rules.

What if the native bridge has a withdrawal delay?

Start the withdrawal early enough to account for the stated delay and any separate claim step. Keep native gas on the source chain for required transactions, and track the withdrawal until the destination funds are claimable and visible.
