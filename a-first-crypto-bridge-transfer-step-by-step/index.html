A First Crypto Bridge Transfer, Step by Step

What to tell a friend before their first crypto bridge transfer: explain the two chains and gas tokens, normal waits, explorer checks, and why seed phrases stay private.

The Crypto Front Page DeskNov 21, 2026 · 05:23 UTC4 min read

A bridge transfer moves tokens from one blockchain to another in two steps: a transaction leaves the source chain, then the transfer settles on the destination chain after the source transaction is final. Tell your friend which chain they are sending from, what they will pay in gas, and what to do while they wait.

3 things to explain

Two chains, two gas tokens

Explain that a bridge transfer involves a source chain and a destination chain, and that gas pays for transactions on the chain where each action happens. This suits someone who can already send crypto but has not followed a transfer across two networks.

On the source chain, they approve or send the transfer using the wallet’s transaction prompt. After that transaction is final, the bridge process completes on the destination chain, where the funds become available. Think of it like a parcel handoff: one carrier accepts it before the next leg can finish.

Take Monad bridge as a concrete example: it is a cross-chain app for moving supported assets between Ethereum and Monad. ETH pays gas to send from Ethereum; MON pays gas to move funds on Monad. The two gas tokens correspond to actions on different chains, so your friend should keep the token needed for the chain they are using.

Waiting is normal; don’t resend

Tell your friend to wait for the source transaction to become final before expecting settlement on the destination chain. This is the step that suits anyone who needs a simple rule for a transfer that appears stuck: check its status before taking action.

A bridge transfer is not one instant movement. The source transaction must finish first; the destination step follows. Timing can vary, so a pause by itself does not mean the transfer failed. Resending because the wallet still looks unchanged starts a second transfer. It can send the same amount again and leave them with two transactions to track.

Show them how to find the transaction in the relevant block explorer. The explorer can show whether the source transaction is pending or final, and whether the destination step has settled. Compare the transaction details with the intended transfer before doing anything else. If the source transaction is still pending, wait. If it is final but the destination has not settled, keep checking the status instead of repeating the transfer.

Most first-timer trouble comes from panic resends and fake support, rather than from understanding the bridge’s two-step sequence. Stay with your friend while they check the transaction, and agree that they will ask you before attempting another transfer.

Never share the seed phrase

Tell your friend that no bridge step requires their seed phrase. The phrase gives access to the wallet; a bridge transaction is signed through the wallet’s normal transaction prompt. This warning suits every first-timer, especially someone who might mistake a convincing support message for part of the transfer.

Make the rule simple: never type the seed phrase into a site, send it in a message, or read it to someone claiming to help. No legitimate person or bridge support process needs it to check a transaction or complete a bridge transfer. If a prompt asks for it, stop and ask you before continuing.

Fake support accounts target people who post publicly about stuck transfers. A stranger may reply or send a direct message, claim to be support, and ask for the seed phrase or direct the person to a site. Tell your friend not to engage. They should bring the problem to you privately first, then check the transaction status together. The transfer moves from one chain to another in order; the phrase stays with its owner.
