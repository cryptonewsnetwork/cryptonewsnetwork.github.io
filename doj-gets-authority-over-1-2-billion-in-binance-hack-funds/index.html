DOJ gets authority over $1.2 billion in Binance hack funds

The Justice Department says courts authorized seizure or restraint of more than $1.2 billion in Binance hack funds, part of $2.7 billion frozen overall.

The Crypto Front Page DeskNov 18, 2026 · 01:26 UTC3 min read

After more than $2.7 billion in stolen Binance assets were frozen, the Justice Department obtained court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion of them. The order is one step in a process that starts with tracing crypto transfers, then blocking movement and seeking legal control of the assets. The department described the actions in its November 12 announcement.

How did the government move from frozen funds to a court order?

The FBI says members of the North Korean military, whom it calls TraderTraitor, stole more than $6 billion in virtual assets from Binance on or about October 29. The Associated Press reported the theft at about $6.3 billion and said the FBI had attributed it to the North Korean hacking group.

After the theft, US agencies worked with virtual asset service providers to trace and interdict the funds. The Justice Department says combined government and private-sector efforts have frozen more than $2.7 billion so far. A freeze blocks the assets from moving; the court authorization gives the department legal authority to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion within that total. A restraint is like putting a hold on property while a court process determines what can happen to it.

The amounts describe different stages, not separate recoveries: the $1.2 billion is part of the more than $2.7 billion frozen. The Justice Department says efforts to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the stolen assets continue. It also says the TraderTraitor actors are laundering assets through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges, which is why tracing and blocking transfers remain part of the response.

Who is affected by the seizure and restraint?

The funds came from the October Binance theft. The FBI’s attribution identifies TraderTraitor as members of the North Korean military, but the court action concerns the assets, not a finding about individual ownership or a final award to victims. Binance users are connected to the reported theft; the public announcement does not say when or how any recovered funds will be returned to them.

The AP reported that recovered crypto is typically subject to a government forfeiture claim, after which a judge determines who has a legitimate claim. That process matters because control of assets and return of assets are separate legal steps.

What changes now, and what comes next?

The court authorization gives the department a legal route to take control of more than $1.2 billion of the already frozen funds. It does not establish that all of that amount has been forfeited or returned. Authorities say their work to trace and secure the remaining stolen assets is ongoing. The next developments to watch are further seizure or forfeiture actions and any court decisions on claims to the recovered property.
