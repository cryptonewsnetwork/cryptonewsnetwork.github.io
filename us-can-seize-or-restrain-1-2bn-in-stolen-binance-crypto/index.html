US can seize or restrain $1.2bn in stolen Binance crypto

The Justice Department says partners froze over $2.7 billion in Binance hack funds, with court authority to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion.

The Crypto Front Page DeskNov 16, 2026 · 01:48 UTC3 min read

US authorities and private-sector partners have frozen more than $2.7 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, and the Justice Department says it has court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion of that total. The department’s November 12 announcement describes a multistep effort: trace the assets, get virtual asset service providers to freeze them, then use court authority to seize or restrain funds.

How did the stolen Binance funds move?

The FBI says North Korean actors it calls TraderTraitor stole more than $6 billion in virtual assets from Binance on or about October 29. It observed the actors converting stolen assets primarily into Ether, then said they were expected to move them through decentralized finance and instant-swap services as part of further laundering.

Those services are part of a route the Justice Department says also involved cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges. Each transfer can move assets between wallets or services, complicating efforts to follow and stop them. The FBI’s cyber alert published October 31 listed wallet addresses linked to the activity and urged service providers to block transactions from those addresses, as well as funds derived from them.

Who helped stop the transfers?

The Justice Department says its National Security Division, Criminal Division and US Attorney’s Office for the District of Columbia worked with law enforcement partners and virtual asset service providers. Those providers helped interdict and freeze the assets before they moved beyond law enforcement’s reach, according to the department.

The figures describe different stages of that effort. More than $2.7 billion has been frozen through combined government and private-sector action. Within that amount, the department has obtained court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion. That legal authority does not mean all the funds have been returned to Binance or victims: the department says tracing, freezing, seizure and forfeiture work is still underway.

What changes, and what happens next?

The court authorization gives the government legal grounds to pursue control of a portion of the frozen assets. The wider freeze may stop providers from processing some transfers, while the FBI’s alert gives them addresses to screen against. The Justice Department says investigators are continuing to trace funds and pursue forfeiture as the actors move assets through services used to launder cryptocurrency.

For Binance and users affected by the theft, the announced seizure authority marks progress in restricting access to some stolen funds; it does not establish that the full amount has been recovered. The next developments to watch are whether authorities secure further seizures or forfeitures and whether service providers block additional transfers tied to the listed addresses.
